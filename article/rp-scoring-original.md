# What is Relative Placement (rp-scoring.com)

Source: https://www.rp-scoring.com/what-is-relative-placement

## Overview

Relative Placement is a judging methodology based on **Bucklin Voting**, where the winner is the competitor that the judges "agree upon the fastest." The system converts judges' opinions into equal rankings to ensure fair dance competition results.

## Judge Requirements

- Odd number of judges minimizes ties
- 7 judges recommended for larger events; 5 acceptable, 9 better
- Random partner divisions: 5 judges each for leads and follows
- **Head Judge**: Scores every contest but scores only count in tiebreaker situations (at larger events). At smaller events, head judge participates as normal judge with same tiebreaker function.

## Preliminary Round Rules

Judges select competitors as:
- **Yes (Y)**: Selected for finals
- **Alternate (A/Maybe)**: Backup candidates

**Sorting Process:**
1. Competitors with majority of judges selecting them for finals rank first
2. Then those with majority selections as finals or alternates
3. Then those without majority votes

**Natural Breaks:** Ranking gaps occur where competitors share identical results. Event organizers use these gaps to determine advancement numbers.

**Tiebreaker Methods (when no natural break):**
- Head judge scores
- Judge deliberation
- Ranking alternates
- Random selection

## Finals Rules

Each judge ranks competitors from first to last place (1 = best).

### Cumulative Majority Method

Judges' scores are cumulated across placement levels:
- Column "1→1": How many judges gave this competitor rank 1
- Column "1→2": How many judges gave rank 1 or 2
- Column "1→3": How many judges gave rank 1, 2, or 3
- ...continues to total contestant count

A box is placed around the **first cumulative total where the competitor obtains a majority of the judges' scores**. The competitor reaching majority fastest wins.

### Tiebreaker Scenarios

**Scenario 1 – Same column, different count:**
- The competitor with the higher count (more judges agreeing) places higher

**Scenario 2 – Same column, same count:**
- Sum the placement scores that make up that majority
- Lower sum total places higher (better individual scores)

**Scenario 3 – Equal sum totals:**
- Include the next lowest placement score for tied competitors
- Continue until differentiation occurs

**Scenario 4 – Identical results:**
- Treat as a two-competitor contest
- Judges' relative rankings of those specific competitors determine placement (battle scoring)

**Scenario 5 – Complete equality:**
- Head judge scores
- Judge deliberation
- Leave as a tie
